Slide 02 • Conceptual Framework

Knowles’ Andragogy Meets Critical Equity

Expanding Classical Adult Learning Theory with Sociocultural Realities

📚

Classical Andragogy (Malcolm Knowles)

  • Self-Concept: Adults move from dependency toward autonomous self-direction.
  • Experience Pool: Life experience constitutes the richest learning resource.
  • Problem-Centred: Motivation aligns with immediate problem-solving needs.
  • Internal Drive: Driven by personal efficacy and vocational mastery.
🔍

The Equity Expansion (Critical Lens)

  • Unequal Safety: Trauma, class, and racism condition who speaks freely.
  • Selective Validation: Traditional curricula privilege dominant cultural capital.
  • Resource Asymmetry: Motivation cannot overcome structural poverty or digital gaps.
  • Intentional Design: Autonomy must be actively structured and scaffolded.
⚡
Table Pair Provocation (3 Mins): "Whose 'lived experience' is most effortlessly validated in your current curriculum—and who has to work significantly harder to be recognized as legitimate?"

Slide 06 • Case Study A

Case Study A: Psychological Safety in Clinical Audiology

From Tutor Compliance to Co-Constructed Norms in Sensory Testing

The Tension

  • Clinical Intimacy: Otoscopy requires close physical peer contact.
  • Vulnerability: Mature learners fear diagnostic incompetence in public.
  • Compliance Trap: Tutor-imposed rules generate silence, not safety.

The Co-Design Shift

  • Day 1 Protocol: Cohort co-authors boundaries for physical feedback.
  • Safe-Fail Zones: Ungraded diagnostic peer rounds before tutors observe.
  • Opt-Out Flags: Non-verbal stop signals during invasive procedures.

The Pedagogical Payoff

  • Clinical Empathy: Experiencing patient vulnerability deepens bed-side care.
  • Shared Accountability: Students actively uphold high clinical standards.
  • Anxiety Drop: 40% reduction in pre-assessment OSCE panic scores.
❓
Facilitator Provocation: "In high-stakes technical training, how do we distinguish between genuine professional standards and arbitrary tutor control?"
Slide 07 • Case Study B

Case Study B: Workload-Sustainable HyFlex in Health

Tripartite Cohort Parity for Adult Nursing & Allied Health Shift Workers

🏫

In-Room Learners

  • Hands-On Simulation: Ward mannequins and physical equipment tasks.
  • Peer Pods: In-person collaborative problem-solving pods.
  • Microphone Duty: Room mics ensure remote peers hear every question.
💻

Live Remote Joiners

  • Digital Anchor: Co-facilitated live chat with dedicated triage student.
  • Breakout Triads: Parallel virtual simulation debriefs.
  • Single Task Canvas: Shared digital whiteboards bridging both spaces.
⏱️

Asynchronous Catch-Up

  • Timestamped Clips: 8-minute core concept summaries, not 2-hour recordings.
  • Collaborative Padlet: Shift-workers post questions answered by cohort.
  • Micro-Checkpoints: 3 self-marking diagnostic questions.
⚡
Workload Golden Rule: Do not teach two classes at once. Anchor the entire session around ONE single collaborative artifact that all three modes interact with simultaneously or sequentially.

Slide 10 • Concrete Implementation Toolkit

Redesign Protocols: Choose Your Practical Track

Actionable Frameworks for Developing vs Stretching Practice

Pathway 1 Protocols (Developing Practice)
  • Apply TILT: Clarify Purpose, Task, and Criteria on next week's slide or rubric.
  • 72-Hour Runway: Release key vocabulary and slides 3 days before lecture.
  • Think-Pair-Share: Insert 90-second whispered partner talk before whole-room sharing.
  • Low-Stakes Exit Ticket: 1-minute anonymous poll checking conceptual clarity.
Pathway 2 Protocols (Refining & Stretching)
  • Unbundle The Brief: Separate technical knowledge verification from written syntax.
  • Co-Constructed Criteria: Spend 15 minutes having students negotiate marking descriptors.
  • Micro-Evaluation Probe: Compare student reasoning depth between written vs audio reflections.
  • Cross-Modular Review: Audit whether prerequisite course sequencing causes drop-off.
⚡
Dignity in Differentiation: "Just as we reject one-size-fits-all curricula for our students, we reject one-size-fits-all professional development for faculty."
