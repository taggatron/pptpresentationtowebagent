The Organelle Matrix — Official Answer Key

GCSE Combined Science / GCSE Biology (Cell Biology Specification Standard)

Total Marks: 21 + 2
1 mark per table box
Sub-Cellular Structure Animal(Eukaryote) Plant(Eukaryote) Bacteria(Prokaryote) Examiner Guidance & Biological Rationale
Nucleus ✔ Present ✔ Present ✖ Absent Bacteria have no nucleus; single circular DNA loop floats free in cytoplasm.
Cell Membrane ✔ Present ✔ Present ✔ Present Found in all living cells to regulate molecular transport in/out.
Mitochondria ✔ Present ✔ Present ✖ Absent Bacteria lack membrane-bound organelles; respiration occurs across the cell membrane.
Ribosomes ✔ Present ✔ Present ✔ Present Found in all living cells for translation & protein synthesis (70S in prokaryotes, 80S in eukaryotes).
Chloroplasts ✖ Absent ✔ Present ✖ Absent Found in photosynthetic green plant cells. (Note: underground plant cells like root hair cells lack them).
Cell Wall ✖ Absent ✔ Present ✔ Present Plant cell walls are made of cellulose; bacterial walls of peptidoglycan. Animal cells NEVER have a cell wall.
Plasmids ✖ Absent ✖ Absent ✔ Present Small circular rings of accessory DNA characteristic of bacteria; often carry antibiotic resistance genes.
Extension Challenge Answer Key (+2 Marks): Structures present in ALL three cell types:
Any TWO of: Cell Membrane (1 mark), Ribosomes (1 mark), or Cytoplasm (1 mark).

⚠️ Critical GCSE Examiner Distinctions & Misconceptions:

1. Bacterial DNA vs Nucleus: A common student error is claiming bacteria have a nucleus. Emphasize that prokaryote means "before nucleus". Their chromosome is a loose circular strand plus plasmids.
2. "Do all plant cells have chloroplasts?": Remind students that root hair cells and onion bulb cells do NOT have chloroplasts because they are underground and receive no sunlight.
3. Cell Wall Chemistry: Plant walls = cellulose (strengthens against turgor pressure). Bacterial walls = peptidoglycan. Animal cells never have cell walls.
4. Respiration in Bacteria: Even without mitochondria, bacteria still carry out respiration using electron transport enzymes on their plasma membrane.
Pedagogical Progression & Differentiation: This matrix functions as an essential diagnostic retrieval check midway through Cell Biology. Target score for mastery: 18/21+. Students achieving <14 should revisit eukaryotic vs prokaryotic distinctions before advancing to microscopy calculations.
