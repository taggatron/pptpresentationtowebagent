Molecular Animation: microRNA Biogenesis & Gene Silencing t = 148s (2:28)
1. Hairpin Transcription & Export Pri-miRNA / pre-miRNA stem-loop hairpins are transcribed in the nucleus and exported to the cytoplasm.
2. Cytoplasmic Dicer Cleavage The Dicer endonuclease enzyme cleaves the hairpin loop into a short (~22 nt) double-stranded miRNA duplex.
3. RISC Complex Activation The single-stranded guide miRNA loads into the RNA-Induced Silencing Complex (RISC) with Argonaute.
4. Target mRNA Silencing Complementary base-pairing cleaves target mRNA or blocks ribosome translation—lowering protein output.
Learner Synthesis Question (Criteria P5 & M3)
Core Assessment Question · P5 (PO2) & M3 (PO3)
“Summarise the role of microRNA (miRNA) in regulating gene expression, and explain how AMT-130 uses this pathway in Huntington’s disease.”
Watch the video clip from 2:28, draft your summary above, then click Reveal Model Answer to check your P5/M3 response.
  • Core Role (Post-Transcriptional Silencing): MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are short (~21–23 nt) non-coding RNAs that fine-tune and silence gene expression post-transcriptionally by binding target mRNA molecules before ribosome translation.
  • Dicer Processing & RISC Loading: Pre-miRNA hairpins are cleaved in the cytoplasm by the Dicer enzyme; the guide strand is loaded into the RNA-Induced Silencing Complex (RISC).
  • mRNA Cleavage vs Translation Block: Guided by complementary base-pairing, RISC either catalytically cleaves and degrades the target mRNA (high complementarity) or blocks ribosome translation.
  • Link to AMT-130 (P5 & M3): AMT-130 delivers an AAV5 vector encoding an artificial miHTT transgene; striatal neurons process miHTT via Dicer and RISC to cleave human HTT mRNA, permanently reducing toxic mutant huntingtin (mHTT) protein.
