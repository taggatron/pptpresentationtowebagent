Why does "throwing a quadrat" violate sound scientific methodology?

1

Subconscious Human Bias

▼
Human choice ruins data: Even unconsciously, students throw quadrats towards colorful patches with flowers, or steer away from stinging nettles, dense mud, or uneven ground.
2

Unequal Sampling Probability

▼
Not truly random: Quadrats can only land within the researcher's physical throwing distance. Distant parts of the habitat have a 0% probability of being sampled.
3

Physical Hazard & Habitat Damage

▼
Safety risk: Metal quadrats are heavy and hazardous when hurled in a working field. Landing with force also crushes delicate flora and displaces ground-dwelling invertebrates.
4

The Scientific Solution (Next Slide)

▼
Coordinate Grid: Lay out perpendicular tape measures to form an (X, Y) coordinate system. Generate coordinates with a random number table/calculator to eliminate all human bias!
