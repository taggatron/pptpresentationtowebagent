Cell vs. Battery

What is the physical and schematic difference between a Cell and a Battery? How does connecting cells in series affect total potential difference?

View Engineering Explanation
A single cell is one electrochemical unit (shown as one long positive line and one short thick negative line). A battery consists of two or more cells joined together in series. In series, the total voltage is the sum of the individual cell voltages: \(V_{\text{total}} = V_1 + V_2 + \dots\)
2

Ammeter vs. Voltmeter Wiring & Resistance

Why must an ammeter have near-zero resistance and be connected in series, while a voltmeter must have extremely high resistance and be connected in parallel?

View Engineering Explanation
An ammeter measures current flowing through a branch; near-zero resistance prevents it from reducing circuit current. A voltmeter measures the potential difference between two points; extremely high resistance prevents significant current from diverting through the meter.
3

LDR (Light Dependent Resistor) Behavior

How does an LDR's resistance change when light intensity increases? Give a real-world engineering application where this is used in an automatic switching circuit.

View Engineering Explanation
Remember: LURD (Light Up, Resistance Down). In bright light, incoming photons free charge carriers, dramatically decreasing resistance. Used in automatic street lamps, smartphone ambient display brightness sensors, and solar trackers.
4

LED (Light Emitting Diode) Polarity

Why does an LED only conduct in one direction? What would happen if you reverse the polarity or omit a series current-limiting resistor?

View Engineering Explanation
Diodes are semiconductor p-n junctions that only conduct when forward-biased. In reverse bias, their resistance is enormous and negligible current flows. Without a series resistor to drop excess voltage, the exponential current in forward bias can overheat and burn out the LED instantly.
5

Fixed Resistor vs. Variable Resistor

How is a variable resistor (rheostat / potentiometer) used in lighting dimmers or volume dials compared to a fixed resistor?

View Engineering Explanation
A fixed resistor provides a constant opposition to current determined by \(R = \rho \frac{L}{A}\). A variable resistor adjusts the effective conductive length \(L\) using a sliding wiper contact, smoothly modulating current (\(I = V/R\)) to adjust bulb brightness or audio gain.
